## Scenario

A figure-style tree of eight bacteria in three familiar groups — Proteobacteria, Firmicutes and Actinobacteria — drawn as a rectangular phylogram, the conventional view when branch length matters. The topology, branch lengths and bootstrap values are illustrative and were not inferred from sequence data.

## Annotation key

- `[layout: rectangular, mode: phylogram]` — right-angle branches, with branch length drawn to scale
- `newick: "..."` — the tree in Newick format; `:0.04` after a name is that branch's length
- `[&&NHX:B=99]` — NHX metadata on an internal node; `B` is the bootstrap support value
- `clade Name = (tip, tip, ...)` — groups tips into a named clade
- `[color: "#0f766e", highlight: both]` — the clade colour, applied to both its branches and a background band
- `scale "substitutions/site"` — the unit printed on the scale bar

## How to read

Only horizontal distance carries meaning: it is evolutionary change, measured against the scale bar. Vertical spacing just keeps tips apart. Two tips whose paths join close to the right are close relatives — E. coli and Salmonella join first. The number beside an internal branch is its bootstrap support: the share of resampled trees that recover that group, so higher means more trustworthy. The coloured bands mark the three clades.
