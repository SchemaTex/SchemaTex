## Scenario

A household is choosing between three electric vehicle acquisition paths: buying new (eligible for a $7,500 federal tax credit with 70% probability, depending on income and vehicle MSRP cap), leasing for three years at a fixed all-in cost, or buying used. The buy-new path carries two sequential chance nodes — credit eligibility then a five-year resale market outcome — while used carries battery-health uncertainty. Payoffs represent net five-year economic value (savings versus equivalent ICE ownership, residual value, minus acquisition and maintenance costs).

## Annotation key

- `decision "…"` — decision node; actor chooses a branch
- `choice "…"` — label on a decision branch
- `chance "…"` — chance node; nature chooses; `prob N` on children must sum to 1
- `end "…" payoff=N` — terminal leaf with net five-year economic value in dollars
- `prob N` — probability (0–1) on a chance branch
- EV rollback: chance = probability-weighted sum; decision = max child EV

## How to read

Roll back from leaves to root. Buy-new EV (with credit branch): 0.55 × 28,500 + 0.45 × 16,000 = **24,075**; without credit: 0.55 × 21,000 + 0.45 × 8,500 = **19,275**. Combined: 0.7 × 24,075 + 0.3 × 19,275 = **22,635**. Lease: flat **14,000**. Used EV: 0.6 × 22,000 + 0.4 × 8,000 = **16,400**. Under these assumptions *Buy new EV* dominates, largely because the 70% credit probability shifts the overall EV substantially upward. Sensitivity analysis on credit eligibility probability and the five-year resale spread is where the real MBA discussion lives.
