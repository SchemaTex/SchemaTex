A hot-work bowtie drawn to the CCPS / Energy Institute *Bow Ties in Risk Management* (2018) convention: the hazard "hot work near flammable materials" sits above the top event "ignition of flammable atmosphere". Two threats (sparks and hot slag, static discharge) reach the top event through their preventative barriers on the left. Two consequences (flash fire, asset loss) leave it through their mitigative barriers on the right. Two escalation factors, one on each side, weaken a named barrier, and each has its own escalation-factor barrier.

Why it works as the exemplar for this type:

- The shape explains the method before anyone reads a label. Causes are on the left, the top event is a single green disc in the centre, and outcomes are on the right. Each threat or consequence band is one straight horizontal chain that turns diagonally only at the last step into the disc.
- Colour names the role, and each role always uses its own colour: threats orange, consequences red, barriers neutral grey, the top event green, escalation factors amber, and the hazard a pale yellow header. Every colour is a light fill with a stronger border of the same hue, so text stays dark and readable.
- Barriers are listed in declaration order from outside to inside, so reading along a band follows the sequence of defences. Every threat and every consequence passes through at least one barrier, which is the CCPS rule the drawing makes visible.
- An escalation factor hangs directly below the barrier it weakens, on a thin grey connector. Its own barrier sits one step further down. That keeps the degradation chain vertical and out of the main band, so it never crosses a threat or consequence line.
- The type scale has three steps: bold title, 12 px element labels, and 9 px for escalation elements and the legend. The legend lists all five roles in one row along the bottom.
