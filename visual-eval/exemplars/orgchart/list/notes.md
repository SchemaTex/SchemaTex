# Org chart, directory list — exemplar target

**Scenario.** The same company as the tree exemplar, but all of it: Lumen Health's forty-one people, five reporting levels deep, drawn the way an HR lead would hand the whole staff list to a new joiner. The tree exemplar draws the leadership; this one draws everybody. That is the reason the variant exists — as boxes and connectors this org runs past four thousand pixels wide and stops being readable — so the drawing has to prove the list wins at that size while still carrying all four things the tree carries: a chief of staff who is not a direct report, an unfilled vacancy, a line that is not a reporting line, and department colour.

**Palette.** Identical to the tree exemplar, deliberately: ink `#12161C` names, slate `#5C6672` job titles, muted `#8B95A2` captions, locations and column heads, hairline `#DDE2E9` panel border and section rules, `#F0F2F6` row rules, guide `#C9D1DB` indent lines, page `#F7F8FA` behind a white panel. Departments keep their hues — Executive `#39435A`, Product `#613E98`, Engineering `#2D6EA3`, Operations `#31865E`, Revenue `#B4762F` — and the one accent outside the set, amber `#B08A2E` on cream `#FDF9EF`, is again spent on exactly one thing, the open position. A person must not change colour when the same chart is redrawn as a list, so the hue is a property of the department name, never of the order the departments happen to appear in.

**Type scale.** Title 22/600, caption 12/400, column head 9.5/600 uppercase with 0.7 tracking, department band 10/600 uppercase with 0.8 tracking, name 13/600, job title 11.5/400, location 11.5/400, report count 11.5/400, dotted-line note 11/400. One name size for everyone. The tree steps its card size by level because a tree has no other way to say who is senior; a list already says it with indentation, so a second encoding would only break the table's rhythm.

**Rows.** One row per person, 28px pitch, no boxes. Each row is a department-coloured initials disc, then five columns that are aligned all the way down the page: name, job title, location, dotted-line report, and a count of everyone below that person. The alignment is the whole point — a directory is read down a column, not across a row, and a title that starts wherever the name happens to end cannot be scanned at all. Only the name is indented; every other column holds its x.

**Department bands.** The department is written once, on a band above the section it opens, in its own hue. Put in a column instead it would print the word ENGINEERING seventeen times down one edge, which says nothing the disc colour and the band have not already said. Bands are set clear of the chief executive's trunk, which runs the full height of the page: a coloured tab at the same x would read as part of that line rather than as a heading.

**Indent guides.** A one-pixel trunk drops from each manager's disc to its last direct report, with a short tick into each. They are drawn a step lighter than the tree's connectors (`#C9D1DB` against `#98A3B1`) because there are five times as many of them on the page; at the tree's weight they turn the left margin into a grid.

**The four conventions, carried without connectors.**

- *Assistant.* Rowan Vale sits under the chief executive at report depth, but the tick into that row ends in a bar instead of at the disc and is drawn in slate rather than the pale guide — the same glyph the tree exemplar's legend uses for a side-hung assistant. Where a report count would be, the row says ASSISTANT. A chief of staff is beside the chain, not a rung of it, and the list has to say so without the tree's sideways stub.
- *Open position.* The vacant mobile platform lead is the only tinted row on the page: cream fill, dashed amber disc with a plus in place of initials, the name in amber. Its report count still reads 2, because two engineers report to an empty seat — the fact an HR reader most needs, and one the tree states far less directly.
- *Dotted-line report.* It gets its own column rather than a line across the page; forty-one rows of crossing dashes would be unreadable. The relationship is written on both of its ends, with the arrow pointing away from the head of design and into the web lead, so it can be found from either row. The column is nearly empty in this org and that is fine: it is the honest place for the relationship, and an org with ten dotted lines fills it.
- *Department colour.* One hue per row, on the disc, repeated on the band and in the legend. As in the tree, the word carries the meaning and the colour does not repeat it.

**Collisions.** The roster is parsed from this directory's own `source.sx`, so the drawing cannot drift from the DSL. Column x positions are derived from text measured in a real browser, not chosen by eye. Nothing is written until every text box has been checked against every other text box, against its own column's right boundary, against every indent guide, and against the canvas edge. Zero collisions.

**Departures from the tree exemplar.** Three, all deliberate. The card is gone — rows on one white panel instead of forty-one bordered boxes, because a border per row is forty-one borders. The level size step is gone, for the reason under *Type scale*. The department moved from a per-row label to a per-section band. Everything else — palette, type roles, accent discipline, legend structure — is held identical so the two variants read as one family.
