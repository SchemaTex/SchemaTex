A technical advance sheet for a six-piece band playing a 40 × 24 ft theatre stage: a drummer on an 8 in riser, two guitar amps, a bass rig with DI, stereo keys, three vocal mics, five monitor wedges plus one in-ear monitor (IEM) mix, two power circuits and a stage box. Below the plan are the input list and monitor output table that go with it. It follows the touring rider convention. The plan is a top-down view with upstage at the top and the audience at the bottom, so stage right (the performer's right) is on the page's left. Inputs are numbered to match the console channels, and wedges are marked with their mix number. The sheet stays on one page together with the input list (ProSoundWeb, https://www.prosoundweb.com/simple-yet-vital-best-practices-in-developing-input-lists-and-stage-plots/2/; Tour Manager Info, https://tourmanager.info/stage-plot-input-list/; StageBuilder Pro, https://stagebuilderpro.com/blog/stage-plot-guide).

Why it works as the exemplar for this type:

- Stage directions are named on all four sides: UPSTAGE, DOWNSTAGE, an AUDIENCE band, and STAGE RIGHT / STAGE LEFT with "performer's right/left" under each. A crew never has to guess the point of view. The deck carries measured 40 ft and 24 ft dimension lines, with each figure set in a gap in its line.
- The plan is drawn to scale at 24 px per ft, so a 40 ft stage is about 960 px wide. Symbols are simple plain line art: labelled amp and keyboard boxes, small "DI" boxes, circles for drum shells with thin grey circles for cymbals, a black dot for each microphone, a lightning-bolt circle for each power drop, and a dotted box for the stage box.
- Two marks carry the meaning, each with a fixed colour. A solid blue circle with a white number is an input channel. An amber trapezoid with "MIX N" inside is a wedge, labelled with who it is for. The IEM uses the same amber. A badge sits right beside the source it belongs to and never covers another symbol.
- Performers are named at their vocal mic ("Maya · lead vocal"), with their wedge directly downstage. The drum fill wedge sits just in front of the riser.
- The input list and monitor output table sit side by side under the plan at full 12 px size, with a dark header row and light alternate rows. Channel and mix numbers are bold in the same blue and amber as the plan marks. Positions use the nine-area codes (USR … DSL), and the legend explains them. 48 V phantom is written as a bold "YES" rather than a colour.
- A one-line legend between the plan and the tables explains every symbol, and the power circuits are repeated as text beside the monitor table.
