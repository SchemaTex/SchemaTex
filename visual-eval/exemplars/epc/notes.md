# Purchase to Pay — EPC exemplar

**Scenario.** A purchase-to-pay run, the process every ERP shop models first. A requisition is raised, purchasing checks budget and supplier, then an **XOR split** picks one of two approval paths (within the spending limit → cost centre owner; above it → finance head) and an **XOR join** merges them back into one approved requisition. A purchase order goes out, then an **AND split** runs two branches at once — the warehouse posts the goods receipt while accounts payable captures the supplier invoice — and an **AND join** waits for both before the three-way match releases payment. Eight events, seven functions, both connector pairs.

**Reading the shapes.** An EPC alternates strictly event → function → event down one centre column. *Events* (passive states) are hexagons pointed left and right; *functions* (active tasks) are rounded rectangles; *logical connectors* are small circles carrying × for exclusive-or, ∧ for and. The yellow ovals and white rectangles hanging off the sides of functions on thin arrowless lines are the extended-EPC decorations: who does the work, and which document the work consumes or produces. They are not control flow and never carry arrowheads.

**Palette (six colours).** Ink `#1F1B2E` for every outline, arrow and label; Lilac `#E9D5FF` events; Mint `#BBF7D0` functions; Amber `#FEF08A` organisational units; Slate `#E5E7EB` connector circles; Muted `#6B7280` hairlines and caption. Information objects are white on white paper. **Type scale** 22 title / 12 node label / 11 side label / 10 caption, Inter with a Helvetica–Arial fallback. One radius (9px), one spacing unit (13px — every gap is a multiple), 1.5px control flow, 1.4px outlines, 1px hairlines.

**Geometry.** Hexagon 46px tall, 15px point inset each side; function 50px tall; connector circle r=14 with the operator drawn as strokes rather than a font glyph so it renders identically everywhere. Splits and joins route **orthogonally** — down out of the connector, along a horizontal bus, then down into the top centre of each branch node, never a diagonal — and arrowheads land on the shape boundary, never mid-line.

**Collisions: 0.** Positions are solved from measured text widths (0.55 × font-size per character), so column offsets, node widths and side attachments are derived, not nudged. Attachments always hang outward — documents left of the centre column, departments right, branch-column functions push theirs away from the centre — so nothing crosses the flow. Every label box was checked against every other label, every drawn segment and the canvas edge.

**Two deliberate departures.** The standard doc records the ARIS reference images as drawing events salmon-pink; lilac separates events from functions just as clearly while leaving red free to mean "rule violation" in the validation overlay. And the doc puts input documents left, output documents right; here documents are always left and departments always right, because one side per *kind* reads faster than one side per *direction* once a diagram has parallel branches.

**Engine gap.** The eEPC adornments drawn here have no grammar in the current parser (it accepts only `event`, `function`, `and`, `or`, `xor` and edges), so they are absent from `source.sx` and exist only in this ideal.
