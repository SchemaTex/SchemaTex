# Evacuation exemplar — Meridian House, Level 2

**Scenario.** One floor of a small office wing: ten rooms off a 22 m corridor with a protected stair at each end. The reader is standing in the Open Office; the sheet shows the way out to the east stair and, as an alternative, to the west stair, then on to the muster point in the car park.

**Colour is meaning, so almost nothing is coloured.** The building is neutral grey — `#8D959D` exterior poché, `#B4BBC2` partitions and door swings, `#F2F4F5` rooms with circulation left white. Only three colours carry information: safe-condition green `#00843D` (exit, first-aid, assembly plates), route green `#00A651` (escape bands), fire red `#C8102E` (extinguisher, call point). The location marker is mandatory blue `#005387` so the reader's own position can't be read as a sign. Text is `#1B2430`, with `#5A646E` and `#8A939C` for secondary and caption levels.

**Type.** Title 18 px semibold, room names 12 px, legend and instructions 11 px, stair names and scale caption 10 px, section headers and footnotes 9 px, Inter/Helvetica throughout.

**Symbols.** Each pictogram is a 24 × 24 solid semantic-colour plate with white knockout artwork per ISO 3864-1: E001/E002 runner and door (mirrored for left-hand exits, plus a threshold bar when it is a final exit), E003 cross, E007 four arrows converging on a square, F001 extinguisher, F005 hand pressing a call point. Exit-direction signs put the ISO 3864-3 supplementary arrow beside the runner on the side of travel, on one plate — an arrow alone is not a complete safety message. Plates are 1.1 m of building at 1:100, i.e. 11 mm printed on A3, above the 7 mm ISO minimum.

**Routes.** Primary is a solid 0.40 m green band, alternative the same band dashed; both carry white chevrons pointing away from the reader (every 2 m on the primary, once per dash on the alternative). Where they share the corridor they run in two offset lanes rather than overprinted, and each lane bends to line up with the door it passes through. The bands run over the stair treads, which is what actually happens.

**Layout was solved, not eyeballed.** A script projects the real metre geometry to the sheet, then tests every text box against every wall, route band, sign plate, door swing, stair flight and panel edge, plus every other text box and the sheet edge: 56 labels, 97 pieces of geometry, 0 collisions. Where a room centre was occupied — the Open Office holds the location marker, the corridor holds five signs and two route lanes — the label moved to measured free space.

**Deliberate departures.** Three things here have no DSL construct today and were drawn anyway because a posted plan needs them: the final-discharge doorways in the outside wall (the grammar can only put a door between two declared rooms), the "IF YOU DISCOVER A FIRE" action panel, and the title-block fields (revision, issue date, posting location). The stair's UP/DN arrow is left off because the escape chevrons crossing the flight already say which way you go, and fire doors are marked by a heavier black leaf and hinge dot instead of printing "EI 30" on the plan — the rating is stated once in the legend, keeping the corridor free of text.
