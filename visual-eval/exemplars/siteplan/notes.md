# Site plan exemplar — 1418 Alder Street, detached ADU

**Scenario.** A homeowner's building-permit site plan for a new detached accessory dwelling
unit (ADU) behind an existing one-storey house, on a 60 × 120 ft lot (7,200 sf) facing Alder
Street. The permit is the highest-stakes reason anyone draws a site plan: a reviewer rejects
the plan if a setback distance is missing, a building sits in an easement, or the coverage
figures are absent. A detached ADU was chosen over an addition, deck or garage because it
carries the whole permit vocabulary at once:
- an existing and a proposed building that must read apart;
- distances from both buildings to every lot line, and between the buildings;
- a recorded utility easement the new building must stay out of;
- driveway, walks and patio that count toward impervious surface;
- utilities, a fence and an existing tree near the work.

A listing sketch, a garden plan or a subdivision concept uses a subset of this kit, so this
drawing sets the rules they copy. The lot is the size of Seattle's SF 7200 zone. The setbacks
(front 20 ft, sides 5 ft, rear 15 ft) and the 10 ft rear easement are typical US residential
values, not one city's code. The plan is in **feet**: `ft` is the DSL's default unit, every US
jurisdiction consulted draws site plans in feet at 1″ = 10′ or 20′, and plats record property
lines in decimal feet.

**Required items, and where each appears.** These are the items the checklists below have in
common, and the place a reviewer finds each one on the sheet.
- *Property lines with bearings and distances* — heavy phantom line (a long dash followed by two short dashes). Each side's bearing and length (N 82°30′00″ E 60.00′, N 07°30′00″ W 120.00′) is set outside the lot parallel to that side; the front one sits in the planting strip between the lot line and the curb.
- *Lot dimensions and area* — the first line of the site data table.
- *Street name and frontage* — ALDER STREET on the centreline of a drawn pavement with curbs; the driveway apron crosses the planting strip to the curb.
- *Existing and proposed structures, told apart* — the existing residence is a grey footprint with a medium outline; the proposed ADU is hatched with the heaviest building outline. Both names carry the permit prefixes (E) and (N) and the footprint size and area.
- *Distance from each structure to the lot lines* — dimension strings from the wall to the lot line: residence 26′ front, 9′ west, 19′ east; ADU 18′ rear, 21′ west, 13′ east; 12′ between the buildings. The same figures, plus the rear of the house and the front of the ADU, are in the setbacks table beside the required values.
- *Setback lines* — dashed lines inside the lot, each labelled with its required distance in the strip it bounds.
- *Easements* — the 10′ utility easement edge as a long-and-short dashed line, labelled inside the strip; the overhead electric line runs through it.
- *Driveway, walks and patio, with material* — concrete surfaces in a light grey tint, each labelled (E) or (N) with its material.
- *Impervious surface and lot coverage* — the site data table: every building and paved surface in square feet, split into existing and proposed, with building coverage 26.4 %, impervious surface 38.9 % and pervious area 61.1 %.
- *Utilities* — water service in blue from the meter in the planting strip to the house (W); sewer lateral from the house to the street centreline (SS); new water and sewer from the house to the ADU (W+SS); overhead electric (OHE).
- *Trees near the work* — the existing 16″ oak, whose canopy comes within 3 ft of the ADU, as a scalloped canopy with its trunk.
- *Fence* — the 6′ wood fence, 1 ft inside the side and rear lot lines, as a thin line with × marks.
- *North arrow and graphic scale* — at the foot of the side panel; the arrow is turned 7.5° so it agrees with the bearings.
- *Project address and legal lot* — the title and subtitle.

Not drawn, because the source cannot state them: owner name, assessor's parcel number (APN),
zoning district, preparer and date. The DSL has no statement for project information, and an
exemplar must not show facts its source does not carry. Also not drawn: drainage arrows, spot
elevations, contours and flood zone, which the siteplan standard excludes (no grading or
drainage); mechanical equipment, which has no DSL marker; and septic or well, which do not
apply to a lot on public water and sewer.

**Layout.** North is up on the sheet; true north is 7.5° east of sheet-up, as on most plats
drawn parallel to the street. The street runs along the bottom, so the front of the lot faces
the reader. The plan fills the left of the sheet and a side panel on the right holds, top to
bottom, the site data table, the setbacks table, the legend, and the graphic scale with the
north arrow. The house stands 26 ft back from the front line. The driveway runs up the east
side to a parking pad beside the house, and the front walk joins it to the front door. The ADU
sits 12 ft behind the house, 18 ft from the rear line and 8 ft clear of the easement, with the
patio between the two buildings and a walk from the parking pad to the ADU door. Text placement
follows three rules:
- property-line labels go outside the lot;
- setback, easement and fence labels go inside the strip they name;
- a dimension figure sits beside its own line, in a stretch no other line crosses.

**Palette** — seven named colours on white paper `#FFFFFF`. The first six are the floor plan
exemplar's, with the same meanings; Stone is added.
- Graphite `#1F2328` — property lines, building outlines, dimension ticks, title, building names, property-line labels, street name.
- Slate `#55606C` — setback, easement, fence, sewer and overhead electric lines; paving outlines; the ADU hatch; tree; plan annotations and dimension figures.
- Pencil `#A3ABB4` — dimension lines, street centreline, table rules, the north-arrow circle, and the dash marking an empty table cell.
- Mist `#EDEFF2` — paved surfaces: street, driveway, walks, patio. In the floor plan it marks built-in counters; here it marks built-in ground surfaces.
- Stone `#D5DAE0` — the existing building's footprint. It is darker than Mist so a building never reads as pavement.
- Water `#4F86B0` — the water service and meter, and the only hue on the sheet. In the floor plan it is glass; across the family, blue means water or glass.
- Caption `#6E7782` — subtitle, footprint sizes, table and section headers, table notes, scale figures.

Utility lines are not coloured by the APWA locate code (red electric, yellow gas, green sewer).
That code is for field marking, and a black-and-white permit print must survive without it, so
utilities are told apart by their tags.

**Type scale** (Inter, falling back to Helvetica Neue / Arial).
- Title 20 px semibold. Subtitle 12 px, Caption.
- Building names 12 px semibold uppercase, letter-spacing 0.7 px; footprint size and area 11 px, Caption, 16 px below.
- Plan annotations (setbacks, easement, fence, paving, tree) 9.5 px uppercase, letter-spacing 0.4 px, Slate.
- Dimension figures 11 px, Slate. Property-line bearings and distances 10.5 px, Graphite.
- Street name 11 px semibold, letter-spacing 1.2 px. Utility tags 8.5 px semibold, in the line's colour.
- Table text 11 px, total rows semibold. Column headers 8.5 px semibold, letter-spacing 0.6 px, Caption. Section headers (SITE DATA, SETBACKS, LEGEND, GRAPHIC SCALE) 9 px semibold, letter-spacing 1 px, Caption — the floor plan's "GRAPHIC SCALE" style. Table notes 10 px, Caption.
- Scale figures 10 px. "N" 12 px semibold.

**Line and hatch grammar.** The drawing scale is 5.8 px per foot. Weight follows legal
importance: the property line first, then the new work, then what exists, then constraints,
then services, then measurements.
- Property line 2.4 px Graphite, phantom dashes. Each side is dashed on its own, stretched so it starts and ends on a long dash, so every corner reads closed.
- Proposed building: 2.4 px Graphite outline over a 0.6 px Slate 45° hatch at 7 px spacing. The hatch stops around the name, so no text sits on hatching.
- Existing building: 1.6 px Graphite outline, Stone fill.
- Setback line 1.1 px Slate, dashes 8 / 5. Easement line 1.1 px Slate, long-and-short dashes 16 / 4 / 4 / 4.
- Utilities 1.3 px, dashes 9 / 3.5 (water in Water blue, sewer in Slate); overhead electric 0.9 px solid Slate. Each line breaks for its tag.
- Fence 0.9 px Slate with a small × about every 28 px.
- Paving outlines 0.9 px Slate over Mist. Curbs 1.1 px Slate; street centreline 0.9 px Pencil, long-and-short dashes, broken for the street name.
- Dimension lines 0.8 px Pencil with 45° Graphite slash ticks 1.3 px, as in the floor plan. They need no extension lines, because each one starts on a wall and ends on a lot line.
- Tree canopy 1.0 px Slate scalloped outline, trunk as a small open circle.

**What is shared with the floor plan, and what differs.** Shared: paper, the six named colours
and their meanings, the typeface and weights, title and subtitle block with a rule below, 45°
slash dimension ticks on Pencil lines with Slate figures, uppercase letter-spaced names with a
lighter size-and-area line beneath, and the graphic scale bar with the split arrowhead in a
circle. Different, because site plans prescribe it: a phantom property line as the heaviest
line in place of wall poché (the solid fill of cut walls), dashed setback and easement lines,
(E)/(N) status by fill and hatch, bearings on the lot lines, utility lines with letter tags,
a side panel carrying the tables and legend that a permit reviewer expects on the sheet, a
north arrow turned to true north, and a scale in feet with 10 ft divisions.

**Numbers.** `scripts/visual-eval/draw-siteplan-exemplar.mjs` parses `source.sx` with the
engine's own siteplan parser, computes every figure on the sheet from that geometry, and exits
non-zero if any typed label disagrees. Regenerate with
`./node_modules/.bin/vite-node scripts/visual-eval/draw-siteplan-exemplar.mjs`.
- *Lot area* is the shoelace area of the parcel polygon, 7,200 sf, checked equal to 60.00 × 120.00.
- *Footprints* are 32 × 40 = 1,280 sf (residence) and 26 × 24 = 624 sf (ADU), so building coverage is 1,904 sf, 26.4 % of the lot.
- *Impervious surface* adds each paved surface clipped to the lot. The driveway apron beyond the front lot line is in the street right-of-way and is not counted, so the driveway is 10 × 50 = 500 sf. The patio is 22 × 10 = 220 sf, the front walk 17 × 4 = 68 sf and the ADU walk 4 × 28 = 112 sf. Existing 2,068 sf plus proposed 736 sf gives 2,804 sf, 38.9 %; pervious area is 4,396 sf, 61.1 %.
- *Nothing is counted twice.* Every pair of surfaces is checked for overlap, and an independent count on a 0.5 ft grid must equal the sum. Percentages are printed only on the three total rows, so no column of rounded percentages fails to add up.
- *Required setbacks* are read from where each setback line is drawn, and must equal the number in its label (the front line at 20 ft reads "20′ front setback").
- *Actual distances* are measured square from each building's walls to each lot line, and each must meet the requirement. The ADU must not overlap the easement strip.
- *Every dimension label* must equal the length it spans. Every building-to-line distance shown on the plan must match the setbacks table, and the 12 ft separation must appear.
- *Every bearing label* must match its line's direction with north turned 7.5° (N 07°30′00″ W for the sides, N 82°30′00″ E for front and rear), read in either direction along the line.

**Collisions.** Every text box is estimated from Helvetica's published character widths (its
AFM metrics) scaled by 1.06 for Inter, because the SVG rasteriser used by the eval does not
report text extents. Each of the 123 text boxes, padded by 2 px, is tested against:
- every other text box;
- the 190 line segments on the sheet: property lines, setbacks, easement, fence and its × marks, utilities, paving and building outlines, the ADU hatch, dimension lines and ticks, curbs, the street centreline, table rules, and the tree canopy sampled as 24 segments;
- the 15 solid symbols: tree trunk, water meter, legend samples, scale bar and north arrow;
- the canvas edge, with a 4 px inset.

The ADU name and size must also lie wholly inside the gap cut in its hatch. The count is 0.

**Departures from `docs/reference/52-SITEPLAN-STANDARD.md`.**
- *Permit-grade, not listing-grade.* The doc positions the diagram for listings and proposals, and lists bearings and permit readiness as non-goals. The exemplar draws what a plan reviewer checks, because that is where a wrong drawing costs something. A listing sketch can omit the tables and bearings and keep the look.
- *Bearings on the property lines.* They are typed as `dim` labels on each lot side, which the grammar already allows; the generator checks each one against the north angle.
- *Computed tables.* The doc says overlays do not compute compliance and dimension labels are user-authored. The drawing computes areas, coverage and setback distances from the geometry and checks the typed labels against them, as a reviewer would.
- *Existing and proposed read from the label.* The DSL has no status attribute, so a label beginning "Existing" draws as (E) with a grey fill, and one beginning "Proposed" or "New" draws as (N) with hatching.
- *Patio as a `zone`.* The DSL has no polygon role for paving; a zone labelled with its material is the closest fit.
- *A `dim` gives the measured points, not where the figure goes.* The doc's example places each dimension line by its coordinates, offset from the lot. Here each `dim` states the two points a surveyor or homeowner measures between (a wall and a lot line, or the ends of a lot line), and the drawing chooses where the figure sits. A figure drawn along a lot line would otherwise sit on the heaviest line on the sheet.
- *Graphic scale only, no written ratio.* `scale 40` draws a 40 ft bar in 10 ft divisions. A stated ratio such as 1″ = 20′ stops being true once the sheet is resized or printed; the bar stays correct.
