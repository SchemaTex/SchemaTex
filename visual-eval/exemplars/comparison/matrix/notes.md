# Comparison exemplar — matrix

**Scenario.** An IT manager shortlists four laptop classes for a field-inspection team against six criteria. One criterion is a measured value (weight in kilograms); the other five are yes, partly or no.

**Marks, not coloured cells.** A yes / partly / no cell holds a small tinted disc with a tick, a wave or a cross in its own colour, and the cell itself stays white or zebra wash. Painting whole cells green, yellow and red turns the table into a heat map where every cell shouts equally. The mark keeps the grid quiet, and it still reads in black and white because the shape carries the meaning as well as the colour.

**Structure.** Option names head the columns over one heavy rule; criteria are row labels in medium weight; alternate rows take a faint wash so a long row is easy to follow across four columns. A key under the table names only the marks the table uses.

**Kit shared by all five comparison exemplars.** Title 22/600 at the top left. Ink `#1B2430` for text and structural rules, slate `#5F6B7A` for secondary text, hairline `#E4E9EE` between rows, wash `#F4F6F9` for zebra rows and the datum column. Colour appears only where it means something: green `#2E7A4E`, amber `#9A6A14` and red `#B03A3A` for yes / partly / no and for pro / con; green again for a winning option; blue `#28598C` and plum `#6B4F8C` for the things being compared — the columns of a T-chart and the two subjects of a double bubble. Body text is 13.5 on a 21 px line, headings 14–17 semibold. Every label is measured and checked to sit inside its cell, and no label overlaps another.
