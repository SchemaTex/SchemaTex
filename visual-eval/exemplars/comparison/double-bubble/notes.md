# Comparison exemplar — double bubble

**Scenario.** A biology teacher's compare-and-contrast map for cell division: mitosis and meiosis, four traits they share, and five that belong to each alone.

**Thinking Maps geometry.** The two subjects are the largest circles, filled solid: blue on the left, plum on the right. The shared traits stand in one column between them, each joined to both subjects; the unique traits fan around the outer side of their own subject on an arc, joined to it alone. Every trait bubble is a true circle of one size, set by the longest wrapped label, so no trait looks more important than another.

**Colour belongs to the subject, not to good or bad.** Unique traits take a pale tint of their subject's colour; shared traits are white with a slate outline, because they belong to neither. Green stays out of this map, since everywhere else in the family it means yes.

**Traits read level across the map.** Unique traits run top to bottom in the order they are written on both sides, so the first trait of mitosis sits level with the first of meiosis ("One division" opposite "Two divisions"). Links run from circle edge to circle edge and are checked to clear every other bubble. Small captions under the three zones — only Mitosis, both, only Meiosis — name them for a reader new to the form.

**Kit shared by all five comparison exemplars.** Title 22/600 at the top left. Ink `#1B2430` for text and structural rules, slate `#5F6B7A` for secondary text, hairline `#E4E9EE` between rows, wash `#F4F6F9` for zebra rows and the datum column. Colour appears only where it means something: green `#2E7A4E`, amber `#9A6A14` and red `#B03A3A` for yes / partly / no and for pro / con; green again for a winning option; blue `#28598C` and plum `#6B4F8C` for the things being compared — the columns of a T-chart and the two subjects of a double bubble. Body text is 13.5 on a 21 px line, headings 14–17 semibold. Every label is measured and checked to sit inside its cell, and no label overlaps another.
