# Comparison exemplar — pros and cons

**Scenario.** A data platform lead weighs moving an on-premises warehouse to a managed cloud service: five pros, three cons. The uneven count is deliberate, because real lists rarely balance.

**Valence is said loudly once, then quietly.** Each column opens with a solid green tick or red cross, its name and its count, over a 2 px rule in the same colour. Each point then repeats the mark as a tinted disc. The loud mark is spent once per column, so the points stay calm enough to read as sentences.

**Rows pair up.** The first pro sits level with the first con, so a reader can hold one against the other. Hairlines between points start at the text, not at the mark, so the marks form a clean column.

**Kit shared by all five comparison exemplars.** Title 22/600 at the top left. Ink `#1B2430` for text and structural rules, slate `#5F6B7A` for secondary text, hairline `#E4E9EE` between rows, wash `#F4F6F9` for zebra rows and the datum column. Colour appears only where it means something: green `#2E7A4E`, amber `#9A6A14` and red `#B03A3A` for yes / partly / no and for pro / con; green again for a winning option; blue `#28598C` and plum `#6B4F8C` for the things being compared — the columns of a T-chart and the two subjects of a double bubble. Body text is 13.5 on a 21 px line, headings 14–17 semibold. Every label is measured and checked to sit inside its cell, and no label overlaps another.
