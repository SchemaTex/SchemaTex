Twelve firsts of human spaceflight, from Sputnik 1 in 1957 to the first commercial crew flight in 2020, placed at their real dates on a proportional year scale. Four turning points (Gagarin, Apollo 11, the first ISS module, Crew Dragon Demo-2) are emphasised. It follows the history and biography timeline found in textbooks and museum panels: one strong horizontal axis, each event on a stem that alternates above and below it, and a short caption at the end of each stem. The dates come from NASA's mission records.

Why it works as the exemplar for this type:

- The axis is a solid dark band with decade labels in white inside it. Stems start at the band's edge, so no stem ever runs through a year label.
- Each caption is a small three-part block: the date in small bold type, the subject in bold ("Apollo 11"), then what happened in regular weight. Text wraps at about 176 px, so captions stay narrow.
- Captions start beside the stem's end dot and read to the right. Near the right edge they flip to the left of the dot. A caption may extend over later events only if their stems are shorter, so a stem never crosses text. Crowded decades like the 1960s become a staircase of stem heights, not overlapping cards.
- Events alternate sides so that the lowest stems are used first. The busy early years spread above and below the axis instead of piling up on one side.
- Emphasis uses one accent colour: turning points get a red filled dot, a thicker red stem and a red date. Ordinary events get a hollow dark dot and a thin stem. The subtitle states what red means, so no separate legend is needed.

Palette: ink #1F2933, muted text #5F6B76, axis band #2B3440 with white #FFFFFF labels, turning-point red #B42318.
