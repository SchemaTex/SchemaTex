# Fishbone exemplar — SMT solder-bridge defect analysis

A process-engineering team asks why solder bridges keep appearing between fine-pitch pins on a surface-mount line. Eighteen causes sit under the six headings Ishikawa's manufacturing checklist uses (Man, Machine, Material, Method, Measurement, Mother Nature), three per heading, and eleven of them carry sub-causes — so the drawing has to survive a cause with two children.

Palette: six mid-dark category hues that stay apart on paper — Man `#1E5FA8` blue, Machine `#0E7C6B` teal, Material `#B25E12` amber, Method `#6A3D9A` violet, Measurement `#B02A37` crimson, Mother Nature `#3F7A28` green. Colour is allowed on exactly two things: a category's lines (bone, cause rules, sub-cause twigs) and the solid pill carrying its name in white. Everything else is neutral slate — the fish outline `#1e293b`, cause text `#334155`, sub-cause text `#475569`, the effect statement `#0f172a`, title and captions — so thirty-odd labels stay calm instead of turning into confetti.

Type scale: title 22, deck 12.5, effect statement 15.5 semibold, category 14, cause 13 at medium weight, sub-cause 12 at regular weight. Stroke weight falls the same way: spine 4, bone 3, cause rule 1.6, sub-cause twig 1.2. Every level is separated on three axes at once (size, weight, colour), so the hierarchy survives being read at arm's length.

The fish is line art. The head is one closed outline — a curved back tapering to a blunt nose — stroked 2.5px in slate over a near-white fill, with one hairline gill curve that meets the outline at both ends and no eye or mouth. The effect statement sits inside it under a small letterspaced EFFECT label. The tail is a forked caudal fin drawn exactly the same way. The spine ends in a solid arrowhead that pierces the head outline. Because the fish itself carries no fill colour, the six category hues are the only saturated ink on the page.

First level: every bone leaves the spine at exactly 60°, leaning away from the head, and the six bones form three mirrored pairs sharing one attachment point, so the spine carries three clean V junctions instead of six scattered joins. Each cause is a horizontal rule that underlines its own label and ends in an arrowhead touching the bone.

Second level: a sub-cause is a short twig — 32px long, at the same 60° as its category bone, 1.2px in the category colour, no arrowhead and no underline. It leaves the cause rule and points back toward the spine, and the label sits at the free end, 6px clear of the twig, on the side away from the bone. The second level is therefore the first level shrunk one step: a twig hangs off a cause rule exactly the way a bone hangs off the spine, and it introduces no new kind of mark.

Spacing: the sub-causes of one cause share a single line and are placed along their parent rule by measured text width, working inward-to-outward from a twig foot 30px short of the bone, with 20px of clear air between neighbouring labels. The cause rule is then drawn long enough to span all its children, so the rule visibly holds the group. Rows are pitched 66px apart — the twig plus its label plus 16px of air — which puts each sub-cause nearer its own parent rule than the next cause down, and that alone makes ownership readable without a box or a tint. Because neighbouring bones are parallel, the wedge between any two is a parallelogram of constant width, so one number (483px) guarantees no label can reach into its neighbour at any height. Every label box was tested against every other label, every line and the canvas edge: zero overlaps.

Two deliberate departures from `docs/reference/13-FISHBONE-STANDARD.md`. §7.1 puts the cause branch on the head side of its bone; this puts it on the tail side, as ASQ, Minitab and Visio templates do, because that wedge is bounded by the previous bone and is therefore a predictable reusable column, whereas the head side is open and lets neighbouring categories collide. §3.1's 80×80 triangle for the effect is replaced by a drawn fish head, because a triangle reads as an arrowhead rather than as the thing the whole metaphor is named after.
