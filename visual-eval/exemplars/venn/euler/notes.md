An Euler diagram of animal groups: vertebrates sit inside animals; mammals, birds and fish sit inside vertebrates and share no members with each other; animals capable of powered flight sit inside animals and partly overlap vertebrates, mammals and birds, but never fish. Short italic examples name what lives in the notable regions: bats (flying mammals), most birds, penguins and ostriches (flightless birds) and insects (flying animals that are not vertebrates). It follows Euler's form (Lettres à une princesse d'Allemagne, 1768; see also Rodgers, "A survey of Euler diagrams", Journal of Visual Languages and Computing, 2014): unlike a Venn diagram, only relations that actually exist are drawn.

Why it works as the exemplar for this type:

- Each relation has one look: a subset is an outline drawn wholly inside its superset, disjoint sets are outlines with a visible gap between them, and a partial overlap is two outlines that cross. Nothing overlaps just because a Venn layout would put it there.
- A region that the curves cannot avoid creating but that has no members is shaded with Venn's diagonal hatching and explained in a one-line key under the diagram. Here, powered flight must cross the vertebrates-only area to reach mammals and birds, but no vertebrate flies without being a mammal or a bird, so that area is hatched rather than left looking inhabited.
- Outlines never touch or run along each other; the outermost set is only as large as its contents and labels need, so the canvas has no wide empty margins.
- Every set name is written inside its own set, in the set's colour and semibold, in a part of the set that no other set covers (Animals in the animals-only margin, Vertebrates below mammals and beside fish), so a name never reads as belonging to a nested set.
- A set that cuts across the hierarchy, here powered flight, is drawn with a dashed outline in a colour outside the taxonomy's palette, so it reads as a property crossing the classes rather than another class.
- Example members are smaller muted italics placed in the region they describe, clear of every outline.
- Fills are light and translucent, darker for the innermost sets, so nesting depth shows as shading; every outline stays solid (or dashed) at 2px so boundaries can be traced where sets cross.
- The subtitle spells out how to read nesting, separation and crossing.
