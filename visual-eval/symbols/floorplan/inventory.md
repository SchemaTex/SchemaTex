# Floor plan symbol inventory

This inventory lists every symbol a Schematex floor plan needs: the doors, windows, openings and stairs the DSL draws as part of walls, the 141 drawings in the engine's symbol catalog (`getSymbolCatalog("floorplan")`: furniture, fixtures, and electrical symbols in US NEC and IEC forms), and a short list of basics the DSL cannot express yet. Evacuation safety signs and stage-plot equipment have their own sets and are not listed here. **Users (90 days)** is the number of distinct ChatDiagram users, out of 1,508 who made a floor plan between June and September 2026, whose generated DSL contains the symbol's name or one of its aliases; a dash means none did, or the name cannot be counted on its own. Word matching over-counts some names: a number marked \* also matches room labels or a longer symbol name ("sink" inside "kitchen-sink", "desk" inside "desk-chair", "double" in "double bed"), so its real count is lower. **The tier rule:** tier 1 is the 30 symbols most people actually draw, after discounting the inflated counts, plus the architectural basics no plan can do without (door kinds, window, opening, straight / U / L stairs); tier 2 is regular but less frequent, roughly 60 users or more (about 4 % of floor-plan users), plus the remaining window types and building elements; tier 3 is rare or niche. The drawings follow the accepted exemplar, `visual-eval/exemplars/floorplan/`, and are generated by `scripts/visual-eval/symbols/draw-floorplan.mjs`.

## Missing from the engine

These are symbols the published conventions treat as basic that the DSL has no word for, so an author either leaves them out or fakes them with a symbol that means something else.

- **Garage (overhead sectional) door.** Nearly every house plan with an attached garage has one, drawn as panel lines across a wide opening with the door's track dashed into the garage ([Engineer Fix](https://engineerfix.com/how-to-draw-a-garage-door-on-a-floor-plan/)); today authors use a wide `opening`, which reads as a missing wall.
- **Closet shelf and rod.** The reach-in closet on residential plans is a built-in shelf line with a dashed hanging rod ([House Plans Helper](https://www.houseplanshelper.com/floor-plan-symbols.html)); `wardrobe` is freestanding furniture, so built-in closets are drawn as a piece of furniture standing in a room.
- **Graphic scale bar.** A stated ratio stops being true once a plan is resized or printed, while a bar stays correct ([US NCS Uniform Drawing System, Module 6](https://www.nationalcadstandard.org/ncs5/pdfs/ncs5_uds6.pdf)); the exemplar draws one and the engine cannot.
- **Ramp and level change.** Ramps with a slope arrow and single steps between floor levels decide whether a shop, school or venue is accessible; the DSL has only full stairs.
- **Water heater and furnace.** Utility rooms are labelled with them ("HWT" on [House Plans Helper](https://www.houseplanshelper.com/floor-plan-symbols.html)), and buyers and inspectors look for them.
- **Wall oven, microwave and cooktop as separate appliances.** Kitchen planning distinguishes them because each needs its own landing area ([NKBA Kitchen Planning Guidelines](https://nkba-ps.com/images/downloads/Awards/nkba_kitchen_planning_guidelines_pre_2023.pdf)); the DSL's `oven` and `cooktop` both become a freestanding `stove`.
- **Railing and half-height wall.** Stair wells, lofts, balconies and kitchen pony walls need a guard line that is not a full-height wall; every DSL wall is full height.
- **Crescent round (cabaret) table.** Meeting and banquet layouts seat only the half of a round table facing the stage ([MityLite](https://mitylite.com/blog/cabaret-style-seating)); `round-table-N` always rings the full circle, so some guests face away from the speaker.
- **Barn door.** A surface-mounted sliding door is common in renovations; `type sliding` draws a glazed two-panel door, which is the wrong material and the wrong position in the wall.
